Professional Experience

Graduate Research Fellow – Rice University

Dr. Michael R. King Laboratory · Department of Bioengineering · Houston, TX

  • Innovating ex vivo CAR T cell manufacturing strategies that leverage mechanical stimuli to enhance activation, persistence, and cytotoxicity at solid tumor sites following adoptive T cell transfer therapy
  • Isolating primary human T cells and conducting advanced biophysical assays to reveal age- and sex-specific differences in membrane composition and mechanical properties to inform personalized immunotherapy strategies
  • Developing a computational model of T cell activation (ODE-based intracellular signaling) to generate predictive insights into how membrane composition and biophysical forces regulate immune cell signaling and function

Graduate Research Fellow (Visiting Scholar) – Vanderbilt University

Dr. Michael R. King Laboratory · Department of Biomedical Engineering · Nashville, TN

MS thesis project conducted in clinical collaboration with Vanderbilt University Medical Center, concurrent with PhD dissertation research. Manuscript in review.

  • Led a translational research project investigating circulating tumor cell (CTC) dynamics in metastatic prostate cancer (mPCa) patients undergoing Pluvicto (177Lu-PSMA-617) radioligand therapy, collaborating closely with oncologists, radiologists, and faculty at Vanderbilt University Medical Center
  • Processed over 100 blood samples from mPCa patients before, during, and after Pluvicto treatment to isolate and immunostain CTCs, enabling temporal analysis of CTC burden and phenotypic changes throughout treatment
  • Developed an image processing algorithm to quantify PSMA protein expression and nuclear features in CTCs to correlate cellular characteristics with clinical markers of treatment response, including PSA levels and radiographic findings
